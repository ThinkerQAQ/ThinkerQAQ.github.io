Consumer and producer surplus are simplified measures of gains from trade. Under strong assumptions, competitive equilibrium can maximize total surplus, but those assumptions fail with externalities, market power, public goods, asymmetric information, and missing markets.

Efficiency and equity are different questions. A policy can increase total surplus while changing its distribution substantially, so welfare analysis should state both efficiency effects and who bears costs or receives benefits.
