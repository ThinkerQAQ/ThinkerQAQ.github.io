Economics studies how people and institutions allocate scarce resources under constraints and incentives. Microeconomics focuses on households, firms, prices, markets, and strategic choice; macroeconomics studies aggregate output, employment, inflation, growth, and policy.

Useful reasoning usually starts with opportunity cost and **marginal** changes rather than averages. Models simplify reality, so conclusions depend on assumptions, time horizon, institutions, expectations, and which variables are held fixed.
