## 1. Production Functions

Production transforms inputs such as labor, capital, land, and technology into goods or services.

A production function describes the relationship between inputs and the maximum feasible output under a given technology, for example:

`Q = f(L, K)`

where `L` is labor and `K` is capital. A production function is a technological relationship, not a cost or profit function.

## 2. Short Run and Long Run

In microeconomics, the short run and long run are not fixed calendar periods:

- **short run**: at least one production input is fixed;
- **long run**: all production inputs can be adjusted.

The relevant horizon therefore depends on the industry and technology.

## 3. Total, Average, and Marginal Product

- Total product `TP`: total output.
- Average product `AP`: output per unit of a variable input.
- Marginal product `MP`: additional output from one more unit of a variable input.

When other inputs are fixed, adding more of one variable input eventually tends to encounter **diminishing marginal product**. This does not mean total output immediately falls; it means the additional output from each extra unit eventually declines.

## 4. Isoquants

An isoquant shows combinations of inputs that produce the same output.

Under common assumptions:

- isoquants farther from the origin represent higher output;
- isoquants do not intersect;
- their shape reflects how easily inputs can substitute for one another.

The marginal rate of technical substitution, `MRTS`, measures the marginal trade-off between two inputs while holding output constant.

## 5. Isocost Lines

An isocost line describes input combinations with the same total cost:

`wL + rK = C`

where `w` is the price of labor and `r` is the price of capital.

- A higher budget `C` shifts the line outward in parallel.
- A change in an input price changes the slope.

## 6. Cost Minimization

For a smooth interior solution, cost minimization commonly satisfies:

`MRTS = w / r`

The technological marginal trade-off matches the market relative price of inputs.

With fixed-proportion technologies, corner solutions, or other constraints, a simple tangency condition may not apply.

## 7. Returns to Scale

If all inputs increase by the same proportion, output may increase:

- by a larger proportion: increasing returns to scale;
- by the same proportion: constant returns to scale;
- by a smaller proportion: decreasing returns to scale.

**Returns to scale** describe the production function. **Economies of scale** describe how long-run average cost changes with output. They are related but not identical; the historical note largely conflated the two, so the distinction is made explicit here.
