Behavioral economics studies systematic departures from the simplest fully rational-choice models. Common findings involve limited attention, default effects, framing, present bias, social preferences, and reference-dependent choice.

Bias labels should not replace causal analysis. Effects vary by context, incentives, experience, and experimental design. Behavioral models are most useful when they generate testable predictions and improve explanation or policy design.
