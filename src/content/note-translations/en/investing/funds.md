## 1. What a Fund Is

A fund pools money from multiple investors and invests it according to the objectives and constraints stated in its legal documents.

Investors own fund shares rather than directly owning every underlying security.

## 2. Classification by Investment Assets

Under current CSRC rules for publicly offered securities investment funds in China, major categories include:

- **equity funds**: at least 80% of fund assets invested in equities;
- **bond funds**: at least 80% invested in bonds;
- **money market funds**: invested in money-market instruments;
- **funds of funds (FOFs)**: at least 80% invested in other fund shares;
- **mixed funds**: portfolios that do not satisfy the main percentage tests above;
- other categories recognized by regulation.

When a fund name indicates a specific investment direction, corresponding investment-percentage requirements also apply. The actual mandate should always be checked in the current prospectus and fund contract.

## 3. Active and Passive Management

- **Active funds** allow the manager to select securities and adjust the portfolio in pursuit of the stated objective.
- **Index funds** generally seek to track a specified index rather than forecast which securities will outperform.

Active is not inherently superior to passive, and passive does not mean low risk. Objectives, fees, style consistency, tracking quality, and actual holdings matter.

## 4. Exchange-Traded and Off-Exchange Access

### Off-exchange funds

Subscriptions and redemptions are typically processed through fund managers, banks, brokers, or other distributors, with prices based on NAV and product rules.

### Exchange-traded funds

Listed funds can trade between investors on an exchange. Market price may be above or below NAV, creating premiums or discounts.

Settlement, creation/redemption, trading units, and timing vary by product and exchange. They should not be summarized with one universal T+1 or T+2 rule.

## 5. ETFs

ETF means exchange-traded open-end fund.

The historical note equated ETFs with passive index funds. That is no longer complete. Traditional ETFs often track indexes, but Shanghai and Shenzhen introduced active-management ETF rules in 2026.

> **ETF describes a fund and trading structure; it no longer implies that the strategy must track an index.**

Common features include:

- exchange listing;
- a primary-market creation/redemption mechanism;
- possible premiums and discounts in the secondary market;
- arbitrage and market-making mechanisms that can help market price stay near NAV, without guaranteeing perfect equality.

## 6. LOFs

A listed open-end fund can combine exchange trading with off-exchange subscriptions and redemptions. It may use either active or passive management.

Creation/redemption mechanics, minimums, and settlement rules vary and should not be copied from old fixed numbers.

## 7. Fund Costs

Common costs include:

- management fees;
- custody fees;
- service or distribution fees;
- subscription, redemption, and trading charges;
- brokerage commissions and bid-ask spreads for listed funds;
- transaction costs inside the fund portfolio.

Current fees should be verified from the latest prospectus, key information document, and distributor disclosures.

## 8. A Fund Is Not Automatically Diversified

Diversification depends on the underlying assets, not the wrapper.

Sector funds, single-country funds, thematic funds, or concentrated strategies can all carry substantial concentration risk. Investors holding multiple funds should also check for overlapping positions.

## 9. What to Evaluate

A more durable sequence is:

1. investment objective and benchmark;
2. underlying assets and sources of risk;
3. active versus index-based approach;
4. fees and trading friction;
5. tracking quality or consistency of active management;
6. liquidity and scale;
7. overlap with the rest of the portfolio.
