### 3.1. Space Layout

#### 3.1.1. Entrance
- The first area after opening the front door is the entrance/foyer.

##### 3.1.1.1. How to Arrange the Entrance
Whether an entrance works well largely depends on storage capacity.

###### 3.1.1.1.1. Furniture
- Shoe cabinet
- Coat hooks

#### 3.1.2. Kitchen
- The place for cooking.

##### 3.1.2.1. How to Arrange the Kitchen
###### 3.1.2.1.1. Open vs. Closed
- Open
  - Advantages
    - Air conditioning while cooking in summer
    - Easy access to items
    - More open space
  - Disadvantages
    - Cooking fumes
    - Tidiness

##### 3.1.2.2. Furniture
###### 3.1.2.2.1. Three Major Items
- Sink for washing dishes
  - Put the sink near a window for good light when washing food and dishes.
  - Leave about 30 cm beside the sink for a drying rack.
- Stove
  - Leave a 70-140 cm preparation/cutting counter between stove and sink.
  - Leave at least 20 cm between stove and wall/refrigerator for plating food.
  - A large kitchen can consider a center island; a common design puts the sink in the island.
- Refrigerator
  - Remember the space needed to open the refrigerator door.

###### 3.1.2.2.2. Three Types of Cabinets
- Base cabinets: including the counter, height is commonly around 85 cm in the note.
  - Shelf cabinet
  - Drawer cabinet
  - Narrow pull-out basket
  - Appliance cabinet
- Wall cabinets: commonly about 37 or 40 cm deep in the note.
  - Swing door
  - Lift-up door
- Tall cabinet: strong storage capacity; can hold refrigerator, oven, microwave, washing machine, water softener, and other large appliances.

###### 3.1.2.2.3. Three Small Items
- Hanging rail: can hold spatulas, ladles, tableware, knives, chopping boards, condiment bottles, pot lids, etc.
- Gas water heater
- Microwave

###### 3.1.2.2.4. Other
- Close-capture range hood
- Pull-out sink faucet
- Kitchen air conditioner
- Kitchen knives: Chinese cleaver + Japanese santoku + Western chef's knife + peeler, scissors, fruit knife.

### 3.2. House Systems

#### 3.2.1. Lighting
Lighting system = brightness design + precise placement.

##### 3.2.1.1. Brightness Design
- Reference illuminance × room area ≈ lumens required for the room.
  - Luminous flux
  - Illuminance
- Lighting color temperature
  - Warm-white lights around 3000K-4000K are commonly used.

##### 3.2.1.2. Precise Placement

#### 3.2.2. Electricity
High voltage/power + low-voltage/signal wiring.

##### 3.2.2.1. Power Wiring
- Wire gauge
  - Determine appliance power, then current, then the required wire specification.
  - Circuit breakers
- Distribution design
  - The original note referenced a local attachment image.
- Outlet locations
  - Entrance: separate automatic/sensor lighting and manual control; reserve an outlet.
  - Kitchen: provide sufficient outlets at multiple heights, including suitable circuits/outlets for high-power appliances.
  - Bathroom: many small appliances require careful electrical layout.
  - Dining room: more than one outlet may be needed for induction cooker, microwave, kettle, toaster, etc.
  - Living room: simplify light switches but provide plenty of outlets, especially near the media cabinet for audiovisual and networking equipment.
  - Bedroom: provide bedside switches/outlets and power for wardrobe lighting.

##### 3.2.2.2. Low-Voltage / Signal Wiring
- The original note describes low-voltage circuits used for signal transmission. Modern building/electrical definitions should follow local electrical codes rather than treating all "weak current" simply as DC below 36V.

#### 3.2.3. Water

##### 3.2.3.1. Water Quality
Water quality here refers to hardness and purity.
Ways recorded in the note: pre-filter + water softener + point-of-use purifier.

##### 3.2.3.2. Water Pressure
Water pressure is the pressure in household pipes, measured in MPa. The original sentence about flow was incomplete.

### 3.3. Renovation Style
#### 3.3.1. Main Materials
Flooring (wood, tile, or stone), wall materials (paint), ceiling materials, doors and windows.

##### 3.3.1.1. Floor
1. Do not divide the floor into too many visual zones.
2. Avoid overly decorative skirting boards.

##### 3.3.1.2. Walls
1. Do not divide wall surfaces excessively.
2. Avoid excessive wall panels or padded walls.
3. Avoid an overly decorative TV feature wall.
4. Avoid random color use.

##### 3.3.1.3. Ceiling
Hide structure.
Hide water/electrical systems.
Hide air conditioning.
Hide lighting.
Hide wireless APs.
Hide fresh-air ducts.
