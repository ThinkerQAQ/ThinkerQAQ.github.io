## 1. What Is a Stock?
- For a company, stock is a financing instrument.
- For an investor, it is an equity investment instrument. It represents ownership, provides a claim on returns, and carries risk.

## 2. Why Invest in Stocks?
- High long-term return potential
- Good liquidity

## 3. Stock Classification

## 4. Stock Market

### 4.1. Listing Venues
- Main Board: mainly larger blue-chip companies. Shanghai Main Board stock codes commonly begin with 60, while Shenzhen Main Board codes commonly begin with 000. Companies listed on the Main Board are often relatively large and established.
- SME Board (historical): the Shenzhen Main Board and SME Board were merged on **April 6, 2021**. Former SME Board securities became Main Board A shares, while their original codes and abbreviations remained unchanged.
- ChiNext: mainly innovative and growth-oriented small and medium-sized companies on the Shenzhen Stock Exchange, with stock codes commonly beginning with 300. These companies can have stronger innovation and growth characteristics, along with higher risk.
- STAR Market: mainly serves technology-innovation companies, with stock codes commonly beginning with 688. It has specialized issuance/listing, investor-suitability, and trading rules and should not be summarized simply as having the "lowest threshold."

#### 4.3.3. Intermediaries

##### 4.3.3.1. Securities Companies
- Institutions that provide services for the issuance and sale of securities
- Follow the Company Law and Securities Law
- Nine major businesses:
    - Securities brokerage
        - Execute securities trades on behalf of clients
        - Client: customer
        - Revenue: commissions
        - Classification:
            - Over-the-counter agency trading
            - Exchange-based agency trading
    - Securities investment consulting
    - Financial advisory services related to securities transactions and securities investment
    - Securities underwriting and sponsorship
        - Underwriting
            - Firm-commitment underwriting
            - Standby underwriting
        - Best-efforts distribution: unsold securities are returned to the company
    - Proprietary securities trading
        - The securities company trades securities for itself
        - Party: securities company
        - Funding source: proprietary funds or legally raised funds
    - Securities asset-management business
        - Content: provide services to investors and seek investment returns
        - Classification:
            - Directed asset management for a single client
            - Collective asset management for multiple clients
            - Special-purpose asset management for clients
    - Margin financing and securities lending
    - IB business
    - Private-equity investment fund business and alternative-investment business

##### 4.3.3.2. Securities Finance Company
- Securities companies can borrow funds or securities from a securities finance company; this is the securities-financing company's refinancing business.
- Establishment:

    - Dedicated securities accounts

            - Refinancing dedicated securities account
            - Refinancing collateral securities account
            - Refinancing securities settlement account
    - Dedicated fund accounts
        - Opened with a commercial bank:
            - Refinancing dedicated fund account
        - Opened with the securities registration and clearing institution:

## 5. Stock-Issuance Systems
### 5.1. Administrative Approval System: Government
- Fully administrative approval
- Relatively low efficiency
- Relatively low transparency

### 5.2. Verification System: Government + Intermediaries
- Emphasizes supervision and review
- Moderate efficiency
- Relatively low transparency

### 5.3. Registration-Based System
- Information disclosure is the core, while exchange review, CSRC registration, and continuing supervision after issuance/listing remain. It does not mean "no government regulation."
- China's capital market fully implemented the stock-issuance registration system beginning in February 2023.

## 6. Dividends
### 6.1. What Are They?
Dividends are a way for listed companies to return value to investors.

#### 6.1.1. "Dividend" vs. "Bonus Distribution"
> Small-fix note: in Chinese market usage, terms corresponding to "dividend," "bonus," and "profit distribution" are often used with overlap, and legal/accounting contexts do not always distinguish them exactly as the original note did. The historical learning record below is preserved.

1. Different meanings: the original note described 股息 as interest on shares and 红利 as a portion of listed-company net profit, with the former relatively fixed for a period.
2. Different beneficiaries: the original note associated 股息 mainly with preferred shareholders and 红利 with ordinary shareholders in the secondary market.
3. Different forms: the original note described 股息 as cash or stock payments and 红利 as cash dividends, bonus shares, or capitalization.

### 6.2. Methods
- Cash dividend
    - Cash distribution
    - Reduces company assets and shareholders' equity and causes a cash outflow
- Bonus shares
    - Issued to existing shareholders; the shareholder's proportional ownership and total value do not automatically change merely because of the split into more shares
- Capitalization
    - Capital reserve converted into share capital; the ownership proportion does not change, while shares outstanding and registered capital increase
- Repurchase
    - A listed company repurchases its own shares

### 6.3. Four Dividend Dates
- Declaration date
- Record date
- Payment date
- Ex-dividend / ex-rights date

## 7. Stock Value
- Par value, commonly RMB 1 in the historical context of this note
- Issue price
    - At par: issue price equals par value
    - At a premium: issue price exceeds par value
    - At a discount: issue price is below par value
- Book value
    - Accounting value
    - `Book value = net assets / shares outstanding`, where `net assets = total assets - liabilities`
- Liquidation value
    - Relevant in liquidation
- Intrinsic value
    - Present value of future stockholder benefits

## 10. Custody and Depository
- Custody means an investor authorizes a securities company to handle securities-rights matters.
- Depository means a securities company relies on a securities registration and clearing institution to handle securities-rights matters.

## 13. Call Auction
- Pricing principles:
    - The price that achieves the maximum executable volume
    - A price at which all buy orders above it and sell orders below it can be executed
    - At that same price, at least one side's orders are fully executed
- ![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TDoct/images/master/1607524669_20201209210400564_13291.png)

## 14. Continuous Auction
- Matching principles:
    - When the highest bid and lowest ask are equal, that price is the transaction price.
    - When a bid is above the currently displayed lowest ask, the displayed lowest ask becomes the transaction price.
    - When an ask is below the currently displayed highest bid, the displayed highest bid becomes the transaction price.

## 15. Market Makers
- What they are:
    - Securities dealers connecting buyers and sellers; market makers quote both bids and asks based on market order flow and their own inventory.
- Revenue:
    - Primarily the bid-ask spread.
- Conditions:
    - Strong capital base
    - Ability to manage inventory
    - Ability to quote accurately

## 17. Characteristics of China's Stock Market
> Much of this section is the original note's summary of the A-share system at that time. Registration-based issuance, delisting, trading, foreign-investor access, and investor structure have continued to evolve; the following is preserved as a historical learning record.

- From the issuance-system perspective, the market historically used an IPO verification system, with issuance cadence influenced by regulators and a dual-track pricing environment.
- In issuance volume, regulators historically influenced the cadence of new offerings. IPO queues could be very long, leading some high-quality companies to list overseas.
- From the pricing-mechanism perspective, new-share issuance historically had a dual-track pricing system.
- From the trading-system perspective, the original note said that "A shares lack a short-selling mechanism." This is too absolute: A shares have margin financing/securities lending, stock-index futures/options, and other short or hedging tools, although access thresholds, eligible instruments, and constraints are significant.
- A-share trading has restrictions, notably price-limit mechanisms and T+1 settlement/trading constraints for stocks.
- The original note described the delisting system as insufficiently complete and weak in punishment; this is a historical judgment and current rules should be checked separately.
- The original note described the cash-dividend mechanism as insufficiently effective and linked this to speculation; this is a historical judgment.
- The original note described a relatively high retail-investor share, insufficient long-term capital, and comparatively strong regulatory intervention; these are historical observations and should not be treated as timeless facts.
