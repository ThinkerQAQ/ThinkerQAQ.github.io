## 1. What Valuation Tries to Solve

Market price can be observed. Economic value has to be estimated.

Valuation does not produce one objectively correct price. It creates a set of explicit assumptions and translates expected future economic benefits into a present value range.

## 2. Price Is Not Value

Market price reflects expectations about:

- future earnings;
- interest rates and risk premia;
- liquidity;
- investor sentiment;
- capital flows;
- company fundamentals.

Valuation asks what future cash flows are worth under a stated set of assumptions.

## 3. Two Common Families of Methods

### Intrinsic valuation

Start with expected future cash flows and discount them to present value. Common methods include DDM, FCFF, and FCFE.

### Relative valuation

Use multiples such as PE, PB, PS, or EV/EBITDA to compare a company with peers, its own history, similar business models, or the broader market.

Relative valuation depends heavily on whether the comparison set is truly comparable.

## 4. Valuation Is Not a Price Forecast

A valuation above the market price does not guarantee that the price will rise.

The estimate may be wrong because of:

- incorrect cash-flow forecasts;
- an inappropriate discount rate;
- deterioration in the business model;
- structurally lower market multiples;
- poor governance or capital allocation.

Valuation is an input to a decision, not a return guarantee.

## 5. Discount Rates

The discount rate reflects both the time value of money and the opportunity cost of bearing uncertainty.

Risk should not be handled by arbitrarily adding a larger number. The model should identify whether uncertainty comes from operations, financial leverage, industry structure, country exposure, currency, or other sources.

## 6. Margin of Safety

Because valuation is assumption-sensitive, a point estimate should not be treated as exact truth.

A better process is to:

1. build a base case;
2. build optimistic and pessimistic cases;
3. stress growth, margins, capex, and discount rates;
4. examine whether the current price remains acceptable across a reasonable range.

Margin of safety is a buffer against model error and unknown risks.

## 7. What Determines Valuation Quality

A complicated model is not automatically a better model. Quality depends on whether:

- the business model is understood;
- revenue and margin drivers are explainable;
- capital requirements are modeled correctly;
- accounting earnings convert into economic cash flow;
- terminal value does not dominate without justification;
- critical assumptions survive stress testing.

## 8. Valuation Should Produce a Range

Public companies face many uncertain variables. A more useful output is:

`Key assumptions + reasonable range + sensitivity`

rather than a target price precise to two decimal places.

The real value of valuation is forcing implicit expectations into the open so they can be tested against the current market price.
