## 3. Understand Types of Exercise

### 3.1. Aerobic and Anaerobic
Exercise requires ATP for energy. ATP can be produced in two ways:
1. Aerobic metabolism: requires oxygen.
2. Anaerobic metabolism: does not require oxygen.
Exercise dominated by aerobic metabolism is aerobic exercise; the opposite is anaerobic exercise.

## 7. Create a Training Plan
### 7.1. Training-Plan Parameters
#### 7.1.1. Training Frequency and Split

- Training frequency: how many times to train per week.
Beginners: 2-3 times per week; intermediate/advanced: 1-2 times per week.
- Split: how to complete one round of training for all muscle groups.
1. No split: full body
2. Two-way split: upper/lower body
3. Three-way split: chest, back, glutes/legs
4. Five-way split: chest, legs, back, arms, shoulders

#### 7.1.2. Intensity
The weight used in training, usually expressed as RM.
RM: the maximum number of repetitions you can complete continuously.
1-6: primarily increases strength, secondarily muscle size.
6-12: primarily increases muscle size, secondarily strength and endurance.
12-+: primarily increases endurance, secondarily muscle size.

[Uncle Zhuo Beginner Training Basics 03: Guide to Choosing Load \- Bilibili](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV19T4y1F76o/?spm_id_from=333.788&vd_source=79c9f80f56384444d88bfb3e4cf579df)
#### 7.1.3. Volume

`repetitions*weight*sets`

Repetitions: prioritize training to failure. Why train to failure? Because failure causes accumulation of lactic acid and other metabolites. These increase the osmotic pressure of muscle cells, water flows into the muscle cells and creates the so-called pump. A stronger pump may stimulate secretion of metabolic hormones such as growth hormone, which is beneficial to muscle growth, and may also stimulate intracellular volume sensors, promote protein synthesis, and inhibit protein breakdown.
Weight: depends on intensity; 8RM is best.
Sets: beginners 1-3 sets; intermediate/advanced 3-6 sets.

#### 7.1.4. Time
##### 7.1.4.1. Rest Between Sets
1 min
##### 7.1.4.2. Rest Between Repetitions
1 min
30-90s. The shorter the rest, the more lactic acid accumulates and the stronger the pump.
#### 7.1.5. Exercise Selection
1. Prioritize compound exercises.
2. Prioritize large-muscle-group exercises.
3. Prioritize free-weight exercises.
4. If you want to gain muscle while losing fat, choose HIIT or long-duration low-intensity aerobic exercise.
#### 7.1.6. Movement Speed
For strength, move fast; for muscle growth, use a moderate speed.
Beginners: moderate-slow.
Intermediate/advanced: moderate.

#### 7.1.7. Muscle Contraction

Eccentric contraction: the process in which muscle fibers **lengthen** while yielding to resistance.
Concentric contraction: the process in which muscle fibers **shorten** while overcoming resistance.
[What Does Eccentric Contraction Mean? Is There an Intuitive Explanation? - Zhihu](https://www.zhihu.com/question/24937215)
Pay attention to and control the eccentric phase.

## 9. Eat Properly
### 9.1. Conditions for Muscle Gain
#### 9.1.1. Positive Nitrogen Balance
Nitrogen balance is the protein needed for basic metabolism: ingested protein is first used to synthesize body proteins that have been broken down.
Positive nitrogen balance is the protein needed for muscle growth: after ingested protein is used to rebuild broken-down tissue proteins, the excess is used to synthesize muscle fibers. Roughly 1.8g protein per kilogram of body weight per day.

#### 9.1.2. Calorie Surplus
Calories are used in order for physiological functions, daily activity, and synthesis/growth.

### 9.2. How Much of the Three Macronutrients to Eat

#### 9.2.1. Calculate Basal Metabolic Calories

1. Mifflin-St Jeor formula
    1. 10*65+6.25*172-5*28+5=1590
2. Katch-McArdle formula
    3. 52.3*21.6+370=1499.68
3. Harris-Benedict formula
    4. 66.47+13.75*65+5*172-6.76*28=1630.94
4. World Health Organization
    5. 15.3*65+679=1673.5

#### 9.2.2. Calculate Activity Metabolism

1. Daily metabolic calories = basal metabolic calories × daily activity level
    1. 1550*1.55=2402.5
#### 9.2.3. Calculate Calories Needed for Muscle Gain / Fat Loss / Muscle Gain + Fat Loss

Fluctuate by 250-500.

1. Muscle gain
    1. Fast: 2402.5+500=2900
    2. Slow: 2402.5+250=2650
2. Fat loss
    1. Fast: 2402.5-500=1900
    2. Slow: 2402.5-250=2150
2. Muscle gain + fat loss
    1. 2402.5-300=2100
#### 9.2.4. Convert Calories Into the Three Macronutrients
Sources of calories:

- Carbohydrates: 4 kcal/g
- Protein: 4 kcal/g
- Fat: 9 kcal/g

Carbohydrates are the most efficient because they can quickly break down into glucose.
##### 9.2.4.1. Protein
###### 9.2.4.1.1. How Much to Eat

###### 9.2.4.1.2. How to Choose

##### 9.2.4.2. Carbohydrates
###### 9.2.4.2.1. How Much to Eat

###### 9.2.4.2.2. How to Choose

Tubers

##### 9.2.4.3. Fat
###### 9.2.4.3.1. How to Eat
Use unsaturated fatty acids as much as possible.

#### 9.2.5. Convert the Three Macronutrients Into Food

1. Food scale
2. Food-nutrition app

Using rice as an example, how many grams of carbohydrates are in one bowl of rice?
A food scale shows one bowl of rice = 160g, and a food-nutrition app shows 100g rice = 26g carbohydrates, so one bowl of rice = 160/100*26=41.6g.

What if there is no food scale? Use the fist-measurement method.

A fist-sized portion of rice is 150g. Since 100g rice = 26g carbohydrates, one fist-sized portion of rice contains `150/100*26=39g`.

A fist-sized portion of meat is also about 150g. Since most meat has about 20g protein per 100g, one fist-sized portion of meat contains `150/100*20=30g`.

Protein: `65*1.8=117`, so you need `117/30=4` fist-sized portions of meat.
Carbohydrates: `65*5=325`, so you need `325/39=8.5` fist-sized portions of rice.

Other common foods:

- One egg: 6g protein
- One carton of milk: 6g protein
- 500g clams contains about 100g meat; 100g meat has about 15g protein
- 100g dried cuttlefish: about 65g protein
- 100g pork ribs: 18g protein
- 100g raw noodles: 74g carbohydrates

### 10.2. Understand Where Muscles Generate Force

1. Muscle location: identify the **origin and insertion** of the muscle and the **direction of its fibers**.
2. Joint function: understand what the muscle does, i.e. **how the joint moves when the muscle fibers contract and lengthen**.
3. Match the movement: see whether the **exercise** matches the joint function controlled by the muscle.

From this analysis we can understand two key points:

1. Which muscle an exercise trains.
In an exercise, identify which joint moves—more specifically, which bone approaches which other bone—then look at the muscles attached to those bones to determine which muscle is trained.
2. How a muscle should be trained.
Identify which joint a muscle attaches across—more specifically, which bone approaches which other bone.

### 10.3. Understand Force-Production Technique

1. The closer a body part is to the torso, the earlier it should generate force? No. The muscle you are training should generate force first.

For example, when training the back: the scapula leads the shoulder, the shoulder leads the elbow, and the elbow leads the hand.

1. Upstream/downstream relationship of the body: the closer to the spine, the more upstream; the farther from the spine, the more downstream.

### 10.4. Deliberate Practice With Light Weight
First use a two-way split, light weight and high reps, repeatedly practicing 4 sets of 20RM to become familiar with the movements, for roughly two weeks to one month.

Then move to moderate weight and lower the repetitions, for roughly two weeks to one month.

Finally, use the method of taking the last set to failure.

#### 10.7.1. Lock the Scapulae

Scapular movements:
1. Elevation
2. Depression
    1. Straighten the arm perpendicular to the floor and press downward.
3. Protraction
    1. Extend the arm parallel to the floor and reach forward.
4. Retraction
    1. Extend the arm parallel to the floor and pull backward.

"Locking" means retraction + depression.

Practice:
1. Do it against a wall.
2. Do it lying down.

### 10.12. Glutes
Squatting movements
#### 10.12.1. Hip Hinge

Joints can move. The simplest movements are flexion and extension. Flexion: bending; extension: straightening.

1. Hip flexion: the thigh moves forward.

1. Keep the lower leg perpendicular to the floor.
2. Push the hips backward.
3. Lean the torso forward while keeping the upper body neutral; you can use a ruler to check.
4. Let the knees bend passively and naturally.

2. Hip extension: the thigh moves backward.

Drive forward forcefully while squeezing the glutes.

3. Practice:
1. Avoid bending the knees: place a stool in front of the knees to block them.
2. Avoid sitting downward: push the hips backward until they touch a wall.
2. Scapular stability: hold a weight plate in the hands.

#### 10.12.2. Bodyweight Squat

Deadlift vs squat: the squat uses a large degree of hip and knee flexion, so it trains the gluteus maximus, hamstrings, and quadriceps; the deadlift uses a large degree of hip flexion, so it mainly trains the gluteus maximus.

1. Feet
    1. Stance slightly wider than shoulder width.
    2. Feet turned outward in a slight "8" shape (the narrower the stance, the more forward the feet point).
    3. Torque.
1. Abdomen
    1. Brace.
1. Scapulae
    1. Stable, but not completely locked?
    2. Retract and depress the scapulae?
1. Spine
    1. Neutral; the lumbar spine should neither hyperextend (stick the butt out) nor flex.
1. Hands
    1. Extend straight forward.

Hip hinge + knee flexion until the thighs are parallel to the floor.

The center of gravity must stay over the center of the arch of the foot.

Torque points outward both while standing up and squatting down.

1. Why can't you squat down?
    1. Limited dorsiflexion.
    2. Butt wink.

## 11. Injury Management
### 11.1. Acute Injury
1. What it is: pain, swelling, heat.

2. How to handle it:
    - Immediately (during inflammation): protection, rest, ice, compression, elevation.
    - 72 hours (after the inflammatory stage): massage, heat, etc.
    - Rehabilitation: use small movements to gradually restore joint range of motion.
