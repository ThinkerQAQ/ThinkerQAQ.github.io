Process management covers creation and termination, scheduling, execution states, context switching, resource ownership, synchronization, inter-process communication, and parent/child lifecycle.

The scheduler chooses runnable tasks according to policy and system state; blocked tasks wait for events such as I/O, locks, or timers. Context switching has costs, but the right concurrency level depends on whether work is CPU-bound or spends time waiting.

Correct designs separate execution concurrency from shared-state synchronization and use IPC primitives that match the communication semantics.
