## 1. What GC Does

Garbage collection reclaims managed heap space occupied by objects that are no longer reachable according to the JVM's liveness rules.

A collector may divide work into tracing, marking, evacuation/copying, compaction, reference processing, remembered-set processing, and cleanup phases.

## 2. Stop-the-World and Concurrent Work

A JVM can pause application threads for some phases and perform other work concurrently with them. “Concurrent collector” does not mean “no pauses”; the important question is the duration/frequency of pauses and concurrent CPU overhead for the workload.

## 3. Young and Old Collections

Generational collectors exploit object-lifetime patterns and may distinguish young/mixed/full-style work. Exact event names and triggers depend on the collector.

## 4. Operational Signals

Watch:

- heap occupancy and live set;
- allocation/promotion rate;
- pause percentiles;
- concurrent GC CPU;
- full/degenerate/fallback events;
- container/native-memory headroom.

High GC frequency is often a symptom of allocation pressure or sizing, not automatically a reason to switch collectors.
