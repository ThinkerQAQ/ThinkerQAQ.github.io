## 1. What Is a Distributed Transaction?

A distributed transaction coordinates one business operation across multiple independently managed resources—for example multiple databases, services, or other stateful systems such as Redis or MongoDB.

A local database transaction can enforce ACID properties inside one transactional resource. Once a workflow crosses resource or service boundaries, atomicity and recovery require additional coordination or application-level design.

## 2. Why Are Distributed Transactions Needed?

A business invariant may span multiple resources. For example, one operation may need to debit an account, create an order, reserve inventory, and publish an event. A partial success can leave the overall business state inconsistent.

The correct solution depends on how strong the invariant is, how much blocking is acceptable, and whether compensation is possible.

## 3. When to Use Which Approach

Strong coordination should be reserved for invariants that genuinely require it because it increases coupling and failure sensitivity.

For workflows that can tolerate delayed convergence, common alternatives include:

- **reconciliation**: periodically compare systems and repair mismatches;
- **monitoring + manual repair**: acceptable for rare, non-critical failures if the audit trail is complete;
- **idempotent retries** and durable workflow state;
- **asynchronous messaging** with eventual consistency.

## 5. High-Level Comparison

| Pattern | Coordination style | Typical trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2PC / XA | Synchronous resource coordination | Stronger atomic commit semantics, but blocking and availability costs |
| TCC | Application-level Try / Confirm / Cancel | Explicit business reservations and compensation; high implementation cost |
| Saga | Sequence of local transactions + compensations | Good fit for long-running service workflows; intermediate states can be visible |
| Reliable messaging | Durable local state + asynchronous message delivery | Eventual consistency; requires idempotency and retry handling |
| Best-effort notification | Retry plus receiver-side query/reconciliation | Useful when the receiver can independently confirm final state |

These patterns are not interchangeable. The business invariant should determine the mechanism rather than starting from a preferred framework.

## 6. Consensus vs. Distributed Transactions

Consensus and distributed transactions address different problems.

- **Consensus** lets a set of distributed participants agree on a decision or replicated log despite failures.
- **Distributed transactions** coordinate a business operation across multiple resources while preserving the required application invariants.

Consensus may be used inside a database or coordination service that participates in a transaction, but it does not by itself make a multi-service business workflow transactional.
