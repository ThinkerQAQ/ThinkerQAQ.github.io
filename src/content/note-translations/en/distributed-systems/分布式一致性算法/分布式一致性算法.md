## 1. What Is a Distributed Consensus Algorithm?

More precisely, these are **consensus algorithms**: protocols that allow distributed nodes to agree on a value, decision, or ordered log despite concurrency and failures.

## 2. Why Is Agreement Difficult?

### 2.1 Concurrent Requests

Multiple clients may issue conflicting writes at nearly the same time. If different nodes accept different orders independently, replicas can diverge.

### 2.2 Replication Delay and Failure

A leader may accept a write before every follower has received it. Nodes can crash or become unreachable while different replicas hold different prefixes of the log.

## 3. Why Do We Need Consensus?

Consensus is a building block for systems that need a single agreed leader, a replicated state-machine log, membership decisions, or other forms of coordinated state.

It is related to consistency models but is not itself the same concept as client-visible consistency.

### 4.2 Paxos, Raft, and ZAB at a Glance

| Property | Multi-Paxos | Raft | ZAB |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main abstraction | Replicated log / state machine | Replicated log / state machine | Primary-backup atomic broadcast |
| Normal write path | Stable leader is commonly used in practice | Leader | Leader |
| Log flow in normal operation | Leader/proposer to replicas | Leader → followers | Leader → followers |
| Leader election | Protocol-specific; not part of Basic Paxos itself | Explicit | Explicit |
| Main design goal | General consensus foundation | Understandability and implementability | ZooKeeper atomic broadcast |

Raft and ZAB both use epochs/terms, quorums, heartbeats, and leader election, but their safety rules and recovery procedures differ. Treat them as distinct protocols rather than interchangeable implementations of the same state machine.

### 4.3 Eventual Dissemination

#### Gossip

[Gossip](/en/notes/distributed-systems/%E5%88%86%E5%B8%83%E5%BC%8F%E4%B8%80%E8%87%B4%E6%80%A7%E7%AE%97%E6%B3%95/%E5%88%86%E5%B8%83%E5%BC%8F%E4%B8%80%E8%87%B4%E6%80%A7%E7%AE%97%E6%B3%95%E4%B9%8BGossip/)

Gossip protocols spread information probabilistically and are useful for membership, failure detection, and eventual dissemination. They solve a different problem from quorum-based consensus protocols such as Paxos or Raft.
